How it works

Four steps from
page to fingertip.

01

Capture

Press a button. The camera photographs whatever text is in front of the device — a textbook, a menu, a sign.

02

Recognize

OCR software identifies every character in the image and outputs a clean text string.

03

Translate

Each character is mapped to its Braille equivalent — a unique pattern of 6 dots.

04

Output

Solenoid pins raise to form Braille cells. Ten characters at a time. Press the button to scroll.

The Braille cell

Each cell has 6 solenoid pins in a 2 × 3 grid. Raised pins form a tactile pattern that encodes one character. The final device has 10 cells in a row — 60 pins total — so the user reads 10 characters at a time and scrolls with the button.

The mission

BrailleVision started with a simple question: why can't a visually impaired student just point a camera at a page and read it by touch?

Commercial Braille displays exist, but they cost thousands. We're building a device that does the same core job for under $100 using commodity hardware: a single-board computer, a camera, and 60 solenoids arranged in 10 Braille cells.

The device is portable, battery-powered, and operated with a single button. Photograph any printed text, and the solenoid pins rise to form Braille characters you can read with your fingertips.
